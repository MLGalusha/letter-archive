# About This Project

## What Is Letter Archive?

Letter Archive is a platform for preserving and exploring historical letter collections. It transforms boxes of old letters into a living, explorable digital archive where visitors can discover the stories, connections, and human experiences contained within.

## Who Is It For?

### The Collector

This site is being built for a collector who has amassed letters from many sources—different eras, places, and people. She wants these letters to:

- **Be seen** - Not sit in boxes, never seeing the light of day
- **Be explored** - Visitors can discover stories and connections
- **Live forever** - A permanent digital home for the collection
- **Potentially attract funding** - If the site is compelling enough, it could support full-time collecting and archiving

### The Visitors

People who come to the site might be:
- History enthusiasts curious about everyday life in past eras
- Researchers looking for primary sources
- Genealogists tracing family histories
- Anyone who loves the intimacy of personal correspondence

## The Vision: A Wise Guide

The ultimate vision is an AI-powered exploration experience that feels like talking to **a wise old soul who knows all the stories**.

Imagine asking:
- "What letters do you have from the Civil War era?"
- "Tell me about someone who experienced loss"
- "Are there any letters between parents and children?"
- "What was happening in 1942?"

And receiving a guided response—not a database query result, but a thoughtful answer from someone who has read every letter and understands the human stories within. The AI guide can:

- **Point you to stories** based on themes, emotions, time periods, or people
- **Read stories to you** - Narrate the letters with context
- **Reveal connections** - Show how letters relate across collections
- **Answer questions** - Use natural language, not search syntax

This is the differentiator: not just a searchable archive, but a **conversational guide** to human history.

## Core Goals

### 1. Preservation
Every letter should be:
- Digitized (high-quality scans of the original handwriting)
- Transcribed (both AI-generated and human-verified)
- Catalogued (dates, authors, recipients, locations, topics)

### 2. Explorability
Visitors should be able to:
- **Search and filter** by date, author, location, collection, topic
- **See the originals** - The handwriting matters; it's part of the story
- **Discover connections** - See how letters relate to each other
- **Follow stories** - Track individuals or events across multiple letters

### 3. Story Connections
This is where it gets exciting:
- A collection might contain letters from one family over decades
- Track events and relationships as they unfold through correspondence
- Discover connections that would never be visible in physical archives
- Use AI to identify themes, emotions, and narrative threads

### 4. Accessibility
The letters should be approachable:
- Transcriptions make handwriting readable
- Context helps visitors understand historical references
- The AI guide lowers the barrier to exploration

## What Success Looks Like

- **Primary goal:** A permanent, beautiful home for the collection that makes exploration compelling
- **Secondary goal:** Enough visitor engagement and attention to attract funding for continued archiving work

The site should feel alive—not like a static database, but like a place where stories live and can be discovered.

## Technical Implications

This vision drives several technical priorities:

| Goal | Technical Need |
|------|----------------|
| See originals | High-quality image storage and viewing |
| Search by many dimensions | Rich metadata extraction and indexing |
| AI guide | Vector embeddings, semantic search, conversational AI |
| Story connections | Relationship tracking between letters, people, events |
| Transcription | AI transcription with human verification workflow |

## Future Possibilities

As the site grows:
- **Collections within collections** - Organize by source, theme, or curator
- **Contributor model** - Others might donate or loan letters
- **Educational partnerships** - Schools, museums, historical societies
- **Public events** - Virtual "reading rooms" or guided tours
- **Funding integration** - Grants, sponsorships, donations

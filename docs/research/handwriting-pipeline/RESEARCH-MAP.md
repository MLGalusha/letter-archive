## Technique dependency chain

The pipeline is a chain of evidence transformations, not one detector:

1. **Bind source identity and coordinates.** Use checksum-bound cohort records and
   preserve source coordinates through every crop and rotation.
2. **Propose page structure and orientation.** Kraken, deterministic projections,
   physical-page boundaries, and rotated passes propose lines/regions. They do not
   own transcript text or word pixels.
3. **Partition selectable ink evidence.** Keep a relatively clean layer and a
   source-supported high-recall superset. Neither is handwriting truth.
4. **Propose word positions.** Use line rhythm, Kraken/reviewer boxes,
   transcript-conditioned positions, residual components, and source-coordinate
   fusion as separate locator evidence. Preserve many-to-many parent regions and
   explicit missing-word states.
5. **Condition recovery, separately from ownership.** A generous word-centered
   recovery box may estimate local color, stroke scale, and direction. Recovered
   pixels become selectable candidates; the box must not auto-own every touched
   component.
6. **Select one semantic word.** Start from a software suggestion, then include or
   exclude exact connected pieces and use a persistent cut when ownership shares a
   connected component. Disconnected pieces may belong to one word.
7. **Resolve unique local ownership, then commit and knock it out.** During a
   local line conflict, every component has at most one provisional owner but may
   be explicitly transferred to a neighboring word. Once stable or deferred,
   store the append-only, hash-bound source-coordinate claim and make it
   irreversible. Later committed words may not reclaim it.
8. **Fit geometry around the selected mask.** Try bounded standard/fragmented
   profiles. Geometry can reject unsafe selections but cannot certify semantics.
9. **Audit the fresh residual.** Derive `claim universe AND NOT claimed` from the
   validated ownership ledger, not from old proposal residuals. Residual ink can
   introduce missed words and blocks page completeness.
10. **Evaluate only after freeze.** Compare ownership, neighbor capture, envelope,
    residual, corrections, and timing against sealed evidence. Preserve metric
    disagreements and failure boards.

### Ordered knockout as an algorithmic research direction

The proposed “do not let selected ink be selected again” mechanism already exists
in `src/word_envelope/sequential_ownership.py`: claims are subsets of one bound
global mask, pairwise disjoint, and removed from later local selectable masks.
`src/word_envelope/sequential_residual_audit.py` independently validates the hash
chain and derives the fresh residual.

The first bounded experiment compared reading, reverse, and confidence-first order
against independent selection and a global exclusive alternative on 77 page-007
words. Forward and reverse order changed 41 transcript-locator component owners
(76 with reviewed locators), so first-touch ownership is unsafe even though
reading order improved the acting gate.

A second sealed audit moved one whole claimed component to every other word on its
fitted line and appeared to find useful transfers in both directions. The later
binding audit proved that the completed masks include split and combined semantic
words, so those gain and direction counts are geometry proxies, not trustworthy
semantic evidence. Unique provisional ownership with explicit transfer remains a
useful interaction hypothesis, but neither reading nor reverse order has been
validly accepted or rejected. Future adjudicated comparisons should include:

- transcript/reading order;
- high-confidence interior words first, then ambiguous boundaries;
- alternating line ends toward the center;
- detached marks after their likely bodies but before a neighboring word can
  absorb them; and
- a bidirectional replay that flags claims whose ownership changes with order.

For every order, freeze the same locator and ink inputs. Keep provisional claims
exclusive but allow an explicit local transfer receipt until the neighboring
conflict is resolved; forbid reclaimed pixels only after commit. Measure final
ownership, transfer/correction count, foreign capture, orphaned detached marks,
shared-component cuts, and residual—not just recovered pixels or per-word IoU.
Early mistakes can make later choices look artificially easy, so order-dependent
disagreement is itself a review signal.

### 4a. Global disjoint component assignment

- Current engine: sibling POC `src/word_envelope/component_assignment.py`.
- Experiment: `scripts/experiment_disjoint_component_ownership.py`.
- Frozen result: `artifacts/disjoint-component-ownership-v1/007-p02-body77/`.

Retained conclusion: score each connected component against every word locator,
assign it once to the best-supported word, and abstain on close competitors before
agent review. Transcript global-exclusive assignment produced 56/77 high-quality
selections versus 33/77 for independent touched-component selection and eliminated
41 duplicate claims. It still had 11 false acting approvals, so it is a default
suggestion surface, not an automatic ownership gate.

Two bounded refinements did not justify a policy change. A 49-configuration score
weight/margin sweep produced a fragile 56→57 gain by changing only two components;
line-held-out selection fell to 51/77. A separate cross-line ambiguity sweep fixed
one foreign component but removed more legitimate target ink, reducing high-quality
words to 55/77 at a 0.22 margin. Preserve both as negative evidence. The next
feature should describe locator/line crossing or local stroke-field support, not
apply a blanket line label or globally retune weights.

An acting-only robust ink-line field now supplies that more specific context.
Global blending was also rejected because it fixed one foreign capture but broke a
same-line word. Restricting support to cross-line disputes on components of at
least 50 pixels changed exactly one of 77 masks at the conservative 0.50/1.5
setting: 378 foreign pixels were withheld from `answer.` with no other mask change,
raising high-quality count 56→57. Treat this as a page-bound candidate for held-out
validation, not a production rule; the aggressive variant introduced a different
false assignment.

## Documentation conflicts and missing links

1. **The navigable ownership research is now Git-durable, but the full generated
   corpus is not.** The central docs and POC implementation/evidence snapshot are
   committed, including every primary record in the current artifact registry.
   Roughly 1.3 GiB of generated POC masks/intermediate rasters and unrelated later
   benchmark/application work remain local. A filesystem loss would still erase
   those uncommitted bytes even though their recorded hashes would expose the loss.
2. **The machine-readable registry covers only the active iteration.**
   `ARTIFACT-REGISTRY.json` validates current paths, hashes, roles, decisions, and
   predecessor IDs, but deliberately does not claim complete legacy coverage.
   Older stream logs and artifact manifests still need incremental registry entries.
3. **Envelope timing is described inconsistently.** The central architecture says
   fitting follows accepted selection, while POC `research/README.md` says final
   fitting should be deferred until page finish. Treat this as an unresolved UI and
   performance decision: selected masks must be frozen either way; preview fitting
   must never block or certify ownership.
4. **Counts use different units.** Queue units, transcript positions, committed
   masks, and fitted words are not interchangeable. Record each explicitly rather
   than reconciling 77/78/100/101 counts by assumption.
5. **Visibility is convention-based.** Private truth and public packets are often
   siblings under one artifact family. A builder should enforce allowed roots and
   reject any public packet whose dependency graph reaches evaluation-only files.
6. **Some early artifacts lack a complete model-call envelope.** Model revision,
   latency, tokens, raw response, prompt hash, and runtime identity are not uniformly
   present. Never claim replayable model behavior from a decision JSON alone.

## Append-only organization for new work

Use this shape for meaningful experiments:

`artifacts/<experiment-family-vN>/<page-or-cohort>/<immutable-run-id>/`

Each run should contain:

- `manifest.json`: schema version, immutable run ID, creation time, evidence role
  (`development`, `held_out`, `diagnostic`, `synthetic`, or `sealed_evaluation`),
  acting visibility, source/config/code hashes, dimensions, predecessor run IDs,
  and exact technique sequence;
- `inputs/` or input references: paths plus file and pixel hashes, never silent
  copies or mutable “latest” links;
- `outputs/`: proposal, conditioned-recovery, suggested-ownership, committed-mask,
  envelope, and residual records with distinct roles;
- `metrics.json`: all declared metrics, denominators, abstentions, and timing
  breakdowns;
- `failures.json`: category, unit ID, exact boxes/masks, metric disagreements, and
  board references for every rejected or falsely accepted case;
- `boards/`: a small, purposeful visual atlas generated from frozen records; and
- `CHECKPOINT.md`: candid conclusion, regressions, next falsifying experiment, and
  explicit `promote`, `retain-diagnostic`, `supersede`, or `reject` decision.

After publication:

1. append one row to [`SOURCE-INDEX.md`](SOURCE-INDEX.md);
2. append the technical narrative to the owning stream log;
3. update this map only when storage topology, promoted dependencies, evidence
   boundaries, or supersession changes;
4. never overwrite a run or reuse its ID; and
5. verify referenced hashes and acting/evaluation separation before another model
   sees the packet.

The highest-value future consolidation tool is a read-only catalog generator that
walks manifests, validates their hashes and dependency paths, rejects evaluation
leaks, and emits an index grouped by technique, evidence role, page/cohort, and
status. It should report missing manifests and orphaned artifacts rather than
silently inventing metadata for them.
